When Floodwater in Your Home Is an Emergency

Floodwater is almost never clean. Rising water from a storm picks up soil, fuel and sewage on its way, so it is treated as Category 3 black water. Gray water from a failed sump pump or an overflowing washer is Category 2, and it turns worse the longer it sits.

Storms do not keep office hours, and neither does dispatch. Nights, weekends and holidays get the same-day response when available, and the first call sorts out the depth, the source and whether power is a risk so the right pumps are loaded.

Why Foothill Runoff Is a Local Risk

In 2014 the Colby Fire burned close to 2,000 acres in the hills above town and left slopes mostly bare, officials said. Storms that year pushed mud against a house on Easley Canyon Road, and several streets were impassable until crews cleared them.

The practical lesson is that runoff here carries silt and rock, not just water. Mud dries into a hard layer inside walls and under floors, so it is cleared early, and it affects coverage too, as the cost notes below explain.

Basement Flooding Cleanup

We will not tell you basements are common in Glendora, because that is not something we can show. The below-grade spaces that flood here are lower levels, sunken garages, half-height rooms and crawl spaces, and they share one problem: water has nowhere to drain except back out the way it came in.

Crews start with submersible pumps and extraction units, working steadily while the ground outside is still wet so walls are not stressed. Wet drywall is cut back above the waterline, mud is shoveled and washed out, and stored boxes are sorted into keep and discard piles on the driveway.

Dehumidifiers and air movers then dry the slab and framing, and moisture meters tell us when the room is ready. A failed sump pump makes it Category 2 at best, so porous items go. Our page on how structural drying works explains why a garage wall can stay damp for days.

Sewage Backup Cleanup

Sewage is Category 3 black water, and safety comes before cleanup. Stay out of the room and keep children and pets away. If you must enter, wear boots, gloves and eye protection. Keep fans and the HVAC system off, because moving air spreads contamination. Hold off until the water has been confirmed clean.

Most Glendora sewer lines are looked after by a Los Angeles County district (CSMD), and 1-800-675-HELP (4357) is the number it staffs around the clock for backup and overflow reports. Responsibility for the private lateral is not addressed on the city's site, so confirm it before assuming a bill is yours.

Our crews wear protective equipment, set containment barriers, run HEPA-filtered negative air machines where needed, and remove carpet, pad and wet drywall that cannot be cleaned. Hard surfaces are cleaned, disinfected and dried. Night calls are handled the same way, and same-day response depends on availability. Nothing is rebuilt until the area is clean.

How a Whole-House Flood Job Runs

When water covers most of a floor plan, the job is sequenced. That way nothing gets wet twice.

  1. Walk-through and safety

    Power, gas smells, depth and contamination are checked first.

  2. Extraction

    Pumps start at the lowest point and work upward through the rooms.

  3. Remove what cannot be saved

    Carpet, pad, wet insulation and swollen board are cut out.

  4. Clean and disinfect

    Mud is washed off framing and slab, and surfaces are treated.

  5. Dry and verify

    Air movers, LGR dehumidifiers and daily readings confirm it is dry.

Crawl Space Water

Soft floors or a musty smell through the vents are often the first hints of water below the house. Insulation sags, the vapor barrier shifts, and the subfloor stays damp long after the surface water is gone.

We pump the space out, remove ruined insulation, and dry the framing and subfloor with dehumidification. If wood stays wet for more than 48 hours, we recommend a mold check, and the mold remediation page lays out what that involves.

If Water Is Still Spreading

Safe to do before crews arrive. Each one limits how much material is lost.

Stop what you can

Shut the main water valve if a supply line is involved. Storm water cannot be shut off.

Check power safely

Cut power only from a dry panel you can reach without stepping in water.

Lift valuables

Move papers and electronics onto furniture or an upper floor.

Stay out of the water

It can carry sewage and sharp debris, so keep people and pets clear.

Document the damage

Shoot wide and close-up pictures of the waterline before moving contents.

Flood Coverage and Local Resources in Glendora

Insurance rules matter before the pumps start. State insurance guidance says a typical homeowners policy leaves out flood and mudflow, which usually need their own policy. The state defines mudflow narrowly, and slope failures or soil creeping downhill are classed separately. Look your property up on FEMA's flood map, and remember a new federal flood policy normally begins 30 days after purchase.

Rebuilt walls may need a permit, and the city page lists no rules, so raise it with Building and Safety early.
