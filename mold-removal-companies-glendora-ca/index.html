How To Choose a Mold Removal Company in Glendora, CA

The right questions sort mold removal companies faster than any ad. Use these to compare bids side by side, including ours.

By the Glendora Water Damage Pros team · Published October 27, 2026

☎ (626) 879-0276See all services

A search for mold removal companies near Glendora returns plenty of names, and names reveal very little. What separates one from another is how they answer a handful of questions about licensing, scope, containment and testing. A company that answers those plainly and in writing is easier to trust than one that quotes a number over the phone.

This guide is a buyer's checklist, not a ranking. We are one option among several, and you should collect at least three bids before you decide.

Start With the License

State rules, per the CSLB website, require a contractor license once labor and materials reach one thousand dollars. Cheaper jobs still call for the right classification if employees or permits are involved. You can check any license at cslb.ca.gov, or phone the board to verify one.

Look up the name and the license status yourself instead of trusting a number on a flyer. Mold work can also fall under separate rules, so put it to the company and the board directly: does mold work in your situation need its own license or classification?

Questions To Ask Every Company

Put the same questions to each bidder and write down the answers. The pattern shows up quickly.

A Written Scope With Containment and Clearance Steps

The scope is the document that lets you compare bids fairly. It should name each affected area, say what comes out and what stays, describe the barriers and air filtration, and state how the work is checked at the end. CSLB advises signing a written contract and nothing less.

Clearance means someone confirms the work area is clean and dry before the walls close up. Ask what the clearance step is, who performs it and what you receive afterward. Our mold remediation scope includes these items, and we welcome side-by-side comparison with any other bid.

Why Testing and Removal Are Usually Separate Jobs

A company that tests a home and then sells you the removal has a reason to find a problem. A company that removes mold and then tests its own work grades itself. Many homeowners hire an independent tester before the job to document conditions and again after to confirm the result.

Testing is optional in many cases. EPA guidance leaves room for professionals to sample when doubt exists. If growth is plainly visible, skip the test and put the money toward the scope. If you do test, ask who pays, who receives the report and who interprets it.

Red Flags That Should Slow You Down

Pressure to sign today

CSLB advises homeowners not to rush after a loss. A real bid will still stand tomorrow.

Cash demands

The board advises avoiding cash payments and never letting payments run ahead of finished work.

A large deposit

The board's cap on a down payment is 10 percent of the contract or one thousand dollars, whichever is less.

No scope, only a price

If you cannot tell what each line pays for, you cannot compare it to another bid.

Promises of a permanent fix

No one can promise mold stays away if moisture returns. Honest firms talk about controlling the water.

Get Three Bids and Call the References

CSLB's advice after a disaster is simple: get three bids, check references, and sign a written contract. Ask for two or three recent customers with a similar job and call them. Ask what the crew did on day one, whether the scope changed and how clean the site was left.

Compare scopes before prices. A low bid that leaves out containment or clearance is a smaller job, not a better deal. If two bids differ widely, ask each company what the other included that it did not.

Nearby-City Example: Covina

In Covina, the city's Building and Safety counter wants contractors to hand over their license along with ID, and owners doing their own work to bring an owner-builder form. That rule belongs to Covina. It shows why a legitimate contractor should have a license at hand. If your home is near Covina, ask the city office what applies to your project.

Glendora Notes: Permits and Who Pulls Them

A page on the city's own website lists Glendora's Building and Safety office as the first stop for permit and inspection questions, with plans checked online. It names no list of repairs that require permits, so you cannot assume either way.

Ask that office directly, then ask each bidder who would handle the paperwork if one is needed. A company that dodges the question has told you something.

Before You Sign: A Checklist

Run each bidder through this list.

  • License status checked at cslb.ca.gov
  • Written scope with rooms, methods, containment and clearance
  • Moisture source identified and who fixes it
  • Testing handled apart from removal
  • Deposit within the CSLB limit, no cash
  • Two or three references called
