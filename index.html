First hour

Water on the floor in Glendora: what to do while you call

Water damage gets worse by the hour, so the first steps matter. Stop the source if you can: close the shutoff valve at a fixture, or the main valve for the house. If the Glendora Water Division office is closed, its page lists weekday hours of 8am to 5pm and no after-hours line, so the main valve at your own property is the tool you can reach.

Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. Lift rugs, books and electronics onto dry surfaces, keep children and pets out, and take photos before anything moves. Those photos become the start of your claim file.

If the water came up from a floor drain or toilet, treat it as sewage. Stay out of it, keep the fans off, and read our flood damage restoration page for what happens next. For standing water that is still deep, emergency water removal is the service that pulls it out.

Glendora sits against the San Gabriel Mountains, and the north edge of town near Big Dalton Canyon and North Glendora has a mud-and-runoff history that flatter cities do not share. The Colby Fire burned nearly 2,000 acres above the city in January 2014, and storms later that year sent mud and debris down Glendora streets.

Policies of that kind generally leave out flood and mudflow, so a foothill owner is better off reading the exclusions before the next storm than after it.

Closer to the Route 66 and Foothill Boulevard corridor, most calls start inside the house: a supply line, a water heater, a washer hose, a roof leak, a condensate line. The City of Glendora Water Division maintains about 224 miles of water mains, but the plumbing inside the wall is yours.

If you are unsure who supplies your address, the water bill is the first thing to check. Azusa Light & Water also serves portions of Glendora, and the bill shows which utility is on your meter.

Sewer backups are a separate case. Most of Glendora's public sewer work runs through a county agency, the Consolidated Sewer Maintenance District, whose 24-hour help line is 1-800-675-4357. Call that line about the main sewer, and call us about what the backup did to the house.

Neighborhoods such as Glendora Village along Glendora Avenue, Alosta south of Ada Avenue and the South Hills each have their own mix of older and newer homes, shared walls and hillside lots. A leak in a shared-wall building can reach a neighbor's unit within hours, which is one reason a business owner on Glendora Avenue should shut off the water first and photograph the damage second.

How we size the job

Three kinds of water, three kinds of response

Industry practice sorts water into three categories, and the category decides what can be dried in place and what has to be removed. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the published reference, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

Water categoryTypical sourceWhat usually happens
Category 1Supply line, roof rain, tank leakExtract, dry in place, monitor with moisture meters
Category 2Washer or dishwasher overflow, sump failureRemove porous materials, clean and sanitize, then dry
Category 3Sewage, rising floodwater, mudProtective equipment, containment, removal of affected materials

Mold is a separate question that follows wet materials left too long. Our mold remediation page covers containment, removal and testing, and water mitigation covers the drying that keeps it from starting.
