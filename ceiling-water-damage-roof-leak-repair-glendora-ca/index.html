Ceiling Water Damage From a Roof Leak: The Repair Order That Works in Glendora

By the Glendora Water Damage Pros team · Published October 27, 2026

A bulging ceiling is holding water

A ceiling that bows downward, feels soft or shows a dark bubble is holding water above the drywall. The weight of a few gallons can bring a section down without warning. Keep people and furniture out from under it.

If the sag is dripping, set a bucket below and relieve the pressure on purpose rather than waiting for a collapse. Put a tarp on the floor, stand to the side of the bulge and not under it, and use a screwdriver to poke a small hole at the lowest point of the bubble. The water drains into the bucket in a controlled stream.

Wear eye protection, since drywall bits and insulation fall with the water. If a light fixture sits in the wet area, do not touch it. Turn off that circuit only from a panel you can reach on dry floor.

The repair order, from entry point to paint

Each step depends on the one before it, and skipping ahead usually means redoing work.

  1. Stop the entry

    A roofer finds the failed flashing, cracked tile or lifted shingle and seals or tarps it. Without this, every later step gets wet again.

  2. Remove trapped water

    Relieve any bulges, mop the floor, and use an extraction unit on soaked carpet or hard flooring below.

  3. Open the ceiling

    Cut out wet drywall past the stain until you reach dry, firm board. Remove wet insulation, since it holds water against the framing.

  4. Dry the cavity

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run until the joists and the underside of the deck read dry on a moisture meter.

  5. Check with imaging

    A thermal imaging camera scans nearby ceilings and walls for cool, wet patches that were not obvious.

  6. Close up and finish

    Drywall, texture and paint come last, after the framing is dry and the roof is confirmed sealed.

What gets removed and what stays

Drywall that is swollen, sagging or stained through is usually replaced. Per the EPA, wallboard can stay and dry in place only when it shows no obvious swelling and its seams hold. A brown ring that has dried and left a firm surface may be treated differently from a soft bubble.

Ceiling tiles are porous, and the guidance is to discard them if they stayed wet or show growth. Fiberglass insulation that got soaked loses its loft and holds water, so it comes out too. Dry insulation a few feet away can often stay.

Cutting a clean opening costs less than it sounds, since drywall is patched in squares. A hole 12 to 18 inches past the visible stain is a common starting point. The inspection tells you where to stop cutting.

The upstairs toilet leak variant

Not every ceiling leak comes from the roof. A toilet supply line or a wax ring that fails upstairs can drip into the ceiling below for hours before anyone sees a stain. The first clue is often a stain in a pattern that follows the bathroom's footprint.

This water starts clean, but a leak at the toilet bowl base can carry waste, so ask a professional to look before you wipe it up with bare hands. If the water came from the bowl or drain, treat it as contaminated until told otherwise. A leak from the supply line is clean water and follows the same dry-in-place rules as a roof leak.

Glendora note: permits for roof work

On its Building and Safety page, Covina names re-roofs and minor mechanical, electrical or plumbing jobs as express permits. Larger projects there go through plan check. That is Covina's rule and not Glendora's, so treat it only as a nearby example of how one city sorts small jobs from larger ones.

The useful step for a Glendora roof is a call to Building and Safety at City Hall, asking if the roof work needs a permit. The division's page names itself as the place to start for permits and inspections, yet it lists no repair rules. Ask before the roofer starts, and ask whether the ceiling work is covered as part of the same job.

Checklist before the repair crews arrive

This list takes about twenty minutes and covers what most adjusters and crews ask for first.

  • Photograph the stain, the bulge and the room contents before you move anything.
  • Move furniture and electronics out of the wet zone and cover what you cannot move.
  • Note when the leak started, whether it follows rain, and which fixtures upstairs were in use.
  • Keep any removed tile or drywall pieces for the adjuster until the claim is settled.
  • Do not paint over a stain until the cavity has been read dry.
  • Ask each trade what it needs to finish before the next one starts.
