Kitchen Water Damage Drying and Rebuild in Glendora, CA

A leak in the kitchen soaks cabinet bases, flooring and wall bottoms out of sight. Our dispatch line answers 24/7 and we dry what can be saved.

☎ (626) 879-0276See all services

Restoring a water-damaged kitchen means pulling water out and drying the cabinets, subfloor, flooring and walls it reached, then repairing what could not be saved. Kitchens are the room where supply lines, drain traps, a dishwasher and a refrigerator ice maker all sit within a few feet of wood and wallboard.

If water is running now, close the shutoff valve under the sink or at the main, and stop the dishwasher. Dispatch answers 24/7, and a same-day visit may be possible, depending on the schedule. Empty the cabinet and photograph the damage while you wait.

Why Kitchens Take the Worst of a Slow Leak

Most kitchen floods start small. A weeping compression nut on a sink supply line drips into a particleboard cabinet base, which wicks the water sideways under the toe kick and into the floor. By the time a cabinet door sticks or a smell appears, the water has often been working for days.

The source decides how the job is handled. A broken supply line or ice maker tube carries clean water, so the materials are usually dried. A dishwasher overflow or a drain line backing up carries gray water, which calls for cleaning and more removal of porous materials.

Know your supplier before a leak happens. Glendora has a city water system, and some addresses are fed by Azusa Light & Water instead, so read the top of a recent bill. That tells you who owns the pipe outside while you deal with the valve indoors.

Signs Behind the Cabinet Doors

Kitchen damage hides behind cabinet faces. These signs point to where the water has been.

Swollen cabinet floor

Particleboard expands and crumbles when wet. A bulging base under the sink means water sat there for a while, and the panel is rarely worth drying.

Lifting or cupped flooring

Edges that rise along the cabinet line show water travelling under the planks or vinyl. The subfloor needs a moisture reading before anything is relaid.

Musty smell with no visible water

Moisture is trapped behind a toe kick or inside a wall cavity. A thermal imaging camera and moisture meter find it without opening everything.

Dark ring on the wall base

A tide line on the wallboard behind the dishwasher or fridge points to a slow line leak, not a one-time spill.

Soft spot underfoot near the sink

The subfloor beneath the cabinet has lost strength. Stop using that area and have it checked before it gives way.

Kitchen Water Safety in the First Hour

Shut the valve first. Under-sink shutoffs turn clockwise, and the main valve is the backup if one will not move. Stop the dishwasher and unplug the refrigerator only if you can reach the plug without standing in water.

Kitchens mix water with outlets, appliance cords and often a panel on the nearby wall. With water around outlets or the panel, keep out of the room and leave switches alone. Turn the breaker off at the panel if it is dry and well clear of any puddle.

If you see sparks, smell burning or catch a gas odor, call 911 and the gas utility before you call us. Sewage from a sink drain backing up is a separate case. For a drain backing up into the sink, the county sewer district runs a line that answers day and night, 1-800-675-HELP (4357).

  • Close the shutoff valve, then open a faucet elsewhere to relieve pressure
  • Move small appliances, food and cleaning supplies out of the wet zone
  • Photograph the cabinet, floor and walls before you clean up
  • Keep children and pets out of standing water near outlets

How We Restore a Wet Kitchen

The order matters because every wet layer under the cabinets keeps the next layer wet.

  1. Trace the source and map the moisture

    We use a moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera to follow the water from the leak to the farthest wet material, including the wall behind the appliances.

  2. Pull the water out

    Extraction units draw water off the floor and from inside the cabinet bases. Toe kicks come off so air can reach the cavity underneath.

  3. Remove what cannot be saved

    Swollen particleboard, soaked insulation and wallboard with open seams are removed. Solid wood face frames and counters often stay.

  4. Dry the cavities and the room

    Air movers blow across surfaces and into cavities, and LGR dehumidifiers pull the moisture out of the room air. Return visits track the readings.

  5. Confirm dry, then rebuild

    Repair starts after readings match dry reference material. Published rules exist for this trade. A fair question to put to any company is whether its technicians work to ANSI/IICRC S500.

Cabinets, Counters and Floors: Save or Replace

Countertops usually stay in place. Stone, quartz and laminate on a plywood deck can sit through drying, though we may loosen the sink and cooktop connections. Plywood cabinet boxes often dry well. Particleboard boxes that swelled rarely return to shape.

Flooring depends on the material. Tile over a sound subfloor can often be dried, while laminate and engineered planks that cupped usually need replacing. Wallboard behind the cabinets stays only when it is unswollen with tight seams. Otherwise it is cut out and replaced.

Restoration is not the same as repair of the plumbing. A plumber fixes the failed line, and we dry and rebuild after. For larger rebuilds, see water damage repair. The first days of extraction are covered under water mitigation, and the drying science is explained on the structural drying page.

What Changes the Scope and Price

Two kitchens with the same leak can need very different work. These factors set the scope, and you hear it explained ahead of any demolition.

Water category

Clean supply water is simpler than gray or black water, which adds cleaning and removal.

Area reached

Water confined to one sink base differs from water that crossed the floor into the dining room.

Cabinet and floor materials

Plywood, particleboard, tile, vinyl and hardwood all respond differently to moisture.

Drying time and equipment

Wall cavities and toe kicks need more equipment days than an open floor.

Rebuild scope

Replacing a cabinet base is smaller than replacing a run of cabinets and flooring.

A burst supply line is the kind of sudden, accidental event policies often cover. Slow drips and neglect often fall outside that, and flood or mudflow typically needs its own flood policy. Read your policy or ask your insurer, and hold on to photos and a written scope.

Mistakes That Make Kitchen Damage Worse

Putting a fan on the cabinet doors only dries the face, not the base. Do not caulk the toe kick shut or lay new flooring over a floor that has not been checked with a meter. Do not run a dishwasher you suspect leaked.

  • Do not toss the damaged panel before photographing it
  • Do not point a home vacuum at puddles
  • Do not wait beyond the one to two day mark the EPA gives for heading off mold

Glendora Kitchens and the Local Rules

On its Water Division page the City of Glendora counts roughly 13,000 meter accounts fed by 224 miles of mains. A break between the main and your meter is the utility's problem, not yours, so confirm on your bill which provider that is.

The city's permit and inspection office sits on East Foothill Boulevard, and its online portal handles plan review. Before cabinets or plumbing are moved, check with the Building and Safety Division on permit needs for the repair. Plumbers need a state contractor license, and the licensing board's website has a lookup you can use before hiring.

Next door, the crews serving San Dimas and homes around Covina run the same steps, though those towns keep their own building offices and utilities.
