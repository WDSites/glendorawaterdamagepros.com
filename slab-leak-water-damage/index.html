Water rising through a slab does not stop on its own, so act now. Our dispatch line is staffed 24/7. Until a crew reaches you, close the main shutoff, stay off wet floors near outlets, get furniture and rugs up off the slab, and keep children and pets in dry rooms.

Slab leak water damage is what a pressurized pipe break under or inside a concrete foundation does to the flooring, baseboards, walls and subfloor above it. The plumber repairs the pipe. We extract the water and dry what it reached.

When Water Is Coming Up From the Floor

A slab leak is urgent for a plain reason: the pipe is under pressure, so water keeps arriving until the supply is closed. Left running, it saturates flooring, wicks into the bottom of the walls and can undermine the soil under the concrete.

If you can hear water running with every fixture off, or the floor is warm and wet in one spot, close the home's main valve first and then call. After-hours calls get the same dispatch line as daytime ones, and a crew is sent out the same day whenever one has an opening.

Electrical and Utility Safety Steps

Water across a floor and a live panel or outlet are a bad combination. Stay out of the room if water reaches wiring, and cut power at the breaker only if the panel is on dry ground and easy to reach. If you smell gas or see sparks, leave and dial 911 or the utility ahead of us.

Never wade through standing water to reach a valve if you cannot tell what is energized.

How Common Are Slab Leaks Here?

We cannot tell you how common slab leaks are in Glendora, because the city does not publish that figure. What we see on calls is the pattern, not a statistic, and the pattern is usually a pipe that corroded, rubbed against concrete or shifted over time. Many local homes are built on slab foundations, but we have no source for the share.

So the honest approach is to recognize the signs rather than guess the odds. A leak in the supply line under a slab is a plumbing repair first. Restoration follows once the water is off.

Signs the Leak Is Under the Floor

Any one of these can have another cause. Two or three together point toward the slab.

Warm spot underfoot

A patch of tile or hardwood that feels warm points to a hot-water line leaking beneath.

Meter keeps moving

With every tap and appliance off, a spinning meter dial or a low-flow indicator means water is going somewhere.

Sound of running water

A faint hiss or trickle in a quiet house, with no fixture open, is a strong clue.

Damp or lifting flooring

Cupping hardwood, curling vinyl or a soft carpet patch can all mark where moisture comes up.

Cracked grout or wet baseboards

Moisture traveling through the slab often shows first at the edges of a room.

What the Cleanup Involves After the Plumber Is Done

Stopping the leak and drying the house are two jobs. The plumber locates the break with listening gear or a slab scan, then repairs the line or reroutes it through the wall. Pipe work can mean opening the slab, and that creates its own water, dust and debris.

Our crew starts by extracting standing water, then maps moisture with a pin meter and thermal imaging camera, and sets air movers and LGR dehumidifiers. The method tracks ANSI/IICRC S500, which is public, so you can ask whichever company you hire whether its crews work to it. Return visits track readings until the flooring and wall bottoms read dry.

  • Extraction of surface water with truck-mounted or portable units
  • Removal of saturated pad, baseboard and loose flooring
  • Drying the slab surface, subfloor edges and wall cavities
  • Containment barriers and HEPA air filtration if the floor is opened or mold appears

Mistakes After a Slab Leak

Do not run fans on a floor you have not checked for contamination, and do not wait for the water bill to settle before calling. Sealing a damp slab under new flooring is the costliest error, since the moisture has nowhere to go.

Tell the plumber that a restoration crew is on the way so the floor is not closed up before readings are dry.
