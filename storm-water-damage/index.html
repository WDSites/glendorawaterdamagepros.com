Storm water damage cleanup means removing rain that has entered a building through the roof, walls, openings or gutters, then drying and repairing what it soaked. Crews extract pooled water, pull out materials that cannot dry, and run drying equipment until readings are normal.

When Rain Coming Inside Is an Emergency

Water running through a ceiling, down a wall or under a door calls for action the same day. Storms do not follow office hours, so dispatch answers nights, weekends and holidays, and same-day response when available covers those hours too. We never promise an arrival time, but the first call sorts out where the water is entering and what is already soaked.

Rain that enters through a roof is usually clean Category 1 water at first. After it passes through dusty attic insulation, a clogged gutter line or a garage floor, it picks up grime and stops being clean. That is why a small drip left overnight can turn into a larger job.

Safety Steps for the First Hour of a Storm Leak

Water and wiring do not mix, and a ceiling that holds water can fail without warning. If a light fixture is dripping or sagging, stay out of that room and switch off its circuit only if the breaker box sits in a dry spot and reaching it means crossing no wet floor.

If you see sparks, smell burning or gas, or a power line is down, call 911 or the utility first, then us. Do not stand under a bulging ceiling, because trapped water can let go all at once.

  • Move rugs, books and electronics out of the drip path
  • Photograph every wet area before anything is moved
  • Keep children and pets out of rooms with sagging ceilings

How Rain Gets Into a Building

Almost every storm leak enters at one of a short list of weak points. Wind pushes rain sideways under lifted shingles, around chimney and vent flashing, and through window frames whose caulk has hardened. Gutters that overflow send water down the wall instead of away from it.

At ground level, rain finds the bottom of a garage door, a sliding door track or a low threshold. Water from these points soaks flooring and the base of the walls first, so damage often shows up far from where the rain actually came in.

  • Roof: lifted shingles, cracked flashing, blocked valleys
  • Walls: window frames, stucco cracks, cracked siding joints
  • Ground level: garage door seals, door thresholds, window wells
  • Drainage: overflowing gutters and downspouts that dump at the foundation

How We Clean Up After Rain Intrusion

The order of work keeps wet materials from soaking dry ones. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published industry standard here, and a reputable company will tell you plainly whether its crews follow it.

  1. Find the water

    A thermal imaging camera and a moisture meter trace how far the rain traveled, including behind walls and above ceilings.

  2. Name the entry point

    We show you where the water got in, so a roofer or window contractor can make the permanent repair.

  3. Extract and remove

    Extraction units pull pooled water, then soaked insulation, ceiling board and carpet pad that cannot dry are cut out.

  4. Run the drying equipment

    Fans and low-grain dehumidifiers go into the wet rooms, and wall and ceiling cavities are opened so air can reach them.

  5. Check and hand off

    Daily readings confirm the materials are dry before anything is closed up or repaired.

Dry It in Place or Tear It Out

Not everything wet needs to come out. Carpet that was soaked by clean rain water can often be dried in place if the work starts quickly, while the pad underneath is often replaced. Our carpet water damage page explains how that call is made.

Ceiling and wall board is trickier. Board that is only damp with intact seams may dry with air movers, but swollen or sagging board is removed and replaced. Cutting a ceiling open costs more up front than waiting, and it also stops insulation from staying wet for weeks.

Mistakes After a Storm Leak

These are the habits that turn a manageable leak into a bigger one.

  • Patching the roof from a wet ladder in the middle of a storm
  • Painting over a stain before the wall is dry
  • Running a household fan on a wet ceiling without finding the source
  • Waiting for the rain to stop before calling

What changes the scope and price

Once the wet areas are measured, we lay out the scope and price ahead of any work. Five things drive the number.

Water category

Clean rain is simpler than water that has crossed grimy insulation or soil.

Affected area

One ceiling bay costs less to dry than rain that tracked through several rooms.

Materials involved

Carpet, ceiling board and insulation decide how much is removed versus dried.

Drying days and equipment

Drying often runs several days, with air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the room.

Rebuild scope

Roof, window and drywall repairs are scoped separately once the structure is dry.
