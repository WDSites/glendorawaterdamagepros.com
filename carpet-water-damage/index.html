Carpet water damage restoration means extracting water from carpet, cushion and the subfloor below, drying all three, and deciding what can stay. The carpet is the visible layer, but the pad underneath holds most of the water and the subfloor holds the rest.

Walk on it as little as possible, lift furniture legs onto foil or blocks, and shut off the source. Calls to dispatch are answered day or night, same-day response comes when available, and no arrival time is promised, so photograph the carpet while you wait.

How Carpet Gets Wet in Glendora Homes

Carpet is rarely hit by one dramatic flood. More often it is an upstairs toilet supply line, a washing machine hose, an AC drain pan overflowing in a hallway closet, or a roof leak that runs down a wall into the bedroom edge. Each leaves a different kind of water behind.

Wall-to-wall carpet is stretched over a tackless strip and sits on a foam or fiber pad. Water moves under the carpet quickly, so a wet spot the size of a dinner plate can be a wet area many times that size beneath.

Roof and rain leaks are common after winter storms in the San Gabriel foothills. If the water came in from above, ceiling and wall materials usually need attention too.

Carpet Safety Steps Before Anything Else

Wet carpet over outlets or near a floor panel is an electrical risk. Stay out of the room, and cut power at the panel only if it is dry and you can reach it without crossing wet floor. Gas smell or sparks means leave the house and phone 911 or the gas utility before anyone else.

If sewage came up through a floor drain or toilet, do not walk through it and do not run fans. When sewage backs up or overflows, the county sewer agency answers calls around the clock at 1-800-675-HELP (4357).

Carpet Versus Pad: Two Different Decisions

Carpet fibers are often fine after clean water if they dry in time. The pad is the weak point. Foam and rebond pads hold water, are cheap to replace compared with the labor to dry them, and are usually discarded even when the carpet above is saved.

The EPA advises drying carpet within 24 to 48 hours and cleaning and drying the subfloor beneath it. Past that, or if mold is visible, the material needs remediation guidance rather than more fans. Carpet that sat in Category 3 water is removed, not cleaned.

What a Wet Carpet Job Looks Like

  1. Extract

    A weighted extraction tool presses water out of the fibers and pad and into the recovery unit. We make several passes.

  2. Lift and float

    We release the carpet from the tackless strip along one wall, fold it back, and remove the pad if it is not worth drying.

  3. Dry the subfloor and carpet

    Air movers push air under the floated carpet, and LGR dehumidifiers lower humidity in the room. Moisture meters confirm progress on each visit.

  4. Clean and treat

    For clean water, a cleaning pass and antimicrobial treatment help the carpet recover. The treatment is a step in the job, not a promise about mold.

  5. Re-lay and re-stretch

    Once readings are dry, carpet goes back over new pad. Put one question to any company you hire: does it follow the published ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard in the field?

Prevention and Mistakes

Rent-store fans on top of the carpet dry the surface and leave the pad wet. A home vacuum is not built for puddles, and do not pull up carpet alone if you smell sewage. Place trays under washers and AC drain pans, and check hoses each year.
